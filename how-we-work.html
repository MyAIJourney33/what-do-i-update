What Do I Update?

How we work

What Do I Update? is a small, independent service. We're not affiliated with CISA, NIST or any company named in our alerts, and we don't run ads. We turn official security notices into plain English and tell you what to update.

Where alerts come from

Device alerts come from CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list: flaws the U.S. government says have been used in real attacks. We check it every hour and alert only on flaws that touch phones, computers, browsers, routers and apps people use at home.

News (outages, breaches, scams) leads with the company's or a government's own notice. We grade every source:

  • A, official: the maker or a government agency. Can back any alert.
  • B, trusted: major news outlets. Can back a Heads up; a Do now also needs an official source.
  • C, other: used only alongside an A or B source. If something is only reported so far, we label it Unconfirmed and take it down after 7 days unless it's confirmed.

How we decide Do now or This week

The same fixed rules apply to everyone. Code applies them, not someone's mood that day:

  • Do now: CISA added the flaw to its list in the last 14 days.
  • This week: added 15 to 45 days ago.
  • Older: shown on that device's page as "if you haven't updated since" the date it was added.

The app and every device page use these same rules, and our release check fails if they ever give different answers.

Fixed versions

When the maker says which version fixes a flaw, the alert shows it as "Fixed in". If the maker hasn't said, we leave it out rather than guess.

Checks before anything goes live

  • Matching tests against real flaws, so alerts reach the right devices and skip the wrong ones.
  • Scans that block trackers, outside calls and download links.
  • A freshness check. If our data is ever more than 48 hours old, the app tells you.

Any failed check stops the update from publishing.

What we can't promise

We can be late, and we can be wrong. Turning on automatic updates on your devices is still the most important thing you can do. We're a second check that tells you when to act today. For anything serious, confirm with the maker's own notice, linked under "Read more" on each alert.
