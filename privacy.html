Privacy and terms

Last updated November 21, 2026

The short version
  • No account, no tracking, no ads and no cookies.
  • The list of things you own stays on your phone. We never see it, store it or sell it.
  • We never send download links. Any message with a link that claims to be from us is a scam.

What stays on your phone

Your device lists, any names you add (like "Mom"), your checkmarks, the alerts you mark as fixed and the breached services you mark as ones you use. They're saved in your browser's storage on this phone and nowhere else. To remove them, go to Settings › Erase everything on this phone.

The backup code in Settings is made on your phone. It only goes where you paste it.

What we send you

Everyone gets the same public files: the list of flaws being attacked, our news notes, support dates and a list of known data breaches. Your phone compares them with your list. That's why we never need to know what you own.

What our web host sees

The app is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like most web hosts, GitHub may keep technical logs, including visitors' IP addresses, to keep its service secure. We don't receive those logs or use them. GitHub's own privacy statement covers them.

Where the information comes from

  • The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's (CISA) list of flaws known to be used in attacks.
  • Makers' security notices and news reports. Every news note names its source.
  • Support end dates from endoflife.date.
  • Breach information from Have I Been Pwned.
